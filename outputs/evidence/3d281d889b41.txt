digital products<!-- --> · <!-- -->Practical guide<!-- --> · <!-- -->3<!-- --> <!-- -->min read</p><h1 class="mt-5 text-4xl font-semibold tracking-tight sm:text-5xl">Start SaaS with one job</h1><p class="mt-6 text-xl leading-8">A straightforward way to scope a first SaaS release around a repeatable customer task, rather than an expanding feature list.</p></header><img alt="A central SaaS workspace connected to separate customer workspaces" width="1200" height="800" decoding="async" data-nimg="1" class="my-10 aspect-[3/2] w-full object-cover" style="color:transparent" src="/images/engineering/saas-products.webp"/><div class="mx-auto max-w-2xl"><p class="text-lg leading-8">A SaaS idea often starts with a familiar problem in a business: orders need approval, service requests need an owner or a team needs a shared record. The challenge is deciding which part can become a repeatable product. A first release should help someone finish a real job, with enough operational support to learn from its use.</p><nav aria-label="In this article" class="my-10 bg-card p-6"><h2 class="text-xl font-semibold">In this article</h2><ul class="mt-3"><li><a class="inline-flex min-h-11 items-center text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline" href="#section-1">Name the recurring task</a></li><li><a class="inline-flex min-h-11 items-center text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline" href="#section-2">Scope a complete journey</a></li><li><a class="inline-flex min-h-11 items-center text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline" href="#section-3">Think beyond the demo</a></li><li><a class="inline-flex min-h-11 items-center text-primary underline-offset-4 hover:underline" href="#section-4">Learn from completed work</a></li></ul></nav><section id="section-1" class="mt-12 scroll-mt-28"><h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold sm:text-3xl">Name the recurring task</h2><p class="mt-5 text-lg leading-8">Describe one user, the event that starts their work and the result they need. For example, a branch manager reviews a purchase request and returns an approval or a reason for rejection. That is more testable than a promise to digitize the entire business.</p><p class="mt-5 text-lg leading-8">Talk through the task with potential users and observe how they do it today. Look for the variations they consider essential and the workarounds they barely notice. A product that fits one imagined workflow can fail at the first real exception.</p></section><section id="section-2" class="mt-12 scroll-mt-28"><h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold sm:text-3xl">Scope a complete journey</h2><p class="mt-5 text-lg leading-8">The first release needs a beginning and an end. For an approval product, that could mean creating a request, assigning a reviewer, recording a decision and letting the requester see it. Reporting can start small if the underlying history is reliable.</p><p class="mt-5 text-lg leading-8">List what stays manual during the pilot. Onboarding an organization by hand may be reasonable; losing track of which organization&#x27;s records someone may access is not. Deliberate scope limits should not weaken access boundaries.</p><ul class="mt-6 list-disc space-y-3 bg-card p-7 pl-12 leading-7"><li>One user group and one recurring task</li><li>A complete path from input to outcome</li><li>Clear separation between customer organizations</li><li>A support contact, data export and recovery plan</li></ul></section><section id="section-3" class="mt-12 scroll-mt-28"><h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold sm:text-3xl">Think beyond the demo</h2><p class="mt-5 text-lg leading-8">Decide who can invite colleagues, change permissions, correct mistakes and close an account. Plan how an organization can retrieve its information. These ordinary lifecycle events often reveal more about a product&#x27;s readiness than the polished main screen.</p><p class="mt-5 text-lg leading-8">If payment is part of the pilot, specify what happens when a subscription changes or a payment fails. Use an appropriate payment provider and verify its supported workflow rather than inventing billing behavior around an attractive pricing page.</p></section><section id="section-4" class="mt-12 scroll-mt-28"><h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold sm:text-3xl">Learn from completed work</h2><p class="mt-5 text-lg leading-8">Choose pilot questions before launch: can users complete the task without help, where do they abandon it, and what information do they repeatedly re-enter? Combine event observations with direct conversations; activity counts alone do not explain why someone returned.</p><p class="mt-5 text-lg leading-8">Use those answers to choose the next release. Sometimes the right next step is a clearer invitation flow or a better export, rather than another dashboard. Expand when the core job is repeatable and the operational responsibilities are understood.</p></section><section class="mt-12 bg-card p-7"><h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold">A useful starting point</h2><p class="mt-4 text-lg leading-8">Build one complete, useful journey. Treat access, support and recovery as part of the product, then let real usage guide the next release.</p><a class="engineering-link mt-5" href="/en/contact">Discuss a similar workflow
